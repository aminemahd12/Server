## What changed in this round

- **Harvesting is allowed.** You can buy a complete PC just to strip it and resell what you don't need. Every budget here is **net**: purchase price + delivery − realistic resale of the parts you don't keep. The cash you must pay first is shown separately.
- **Resale values come from sold listings.** eBay blocks every request from this environment, so sold prices come from **Tradera**, a Swedish eBay-style site that shows real sale prices and dates (sales from 26 Aug–10 Oct 2026, converted at the ECB rate of 11.1675 SEK/EUR). They are cross-checked with willhaben ads marked *reserviert* and capped at the current Austrian/German asking-price median, because you would compete with those asks. Two or three independent checks re-derived every value. Selling with willhaben PayLivery costs a private seller nothing.
- **willhaben's buyer-protection fee is now counted.** willhaben charges the buyer at least €0.99, from 4.5% of the item price (5% in its own example), on every PayLivery purchase. Every build below includes it at 5% as a separate line, which raises most totals by €5–30.
- **Pickup in Klagenfurt and Villach now counts**, as you asked (marked *pickup*). Everything else must ship to Villach; pickup-only ads elsewhere are still excluded.
- **Routes that start from a complete PC are tagged** *(prebuilt + upgrade)* or *(prebuilt, stripped)*, because you prefer buying a prebuilt. Every route is still ranked by net cost.
- **Performance is sustained performance.** For 24/7 use in a warm room the CPU's power is capped, so multi-core scores are lower than PassMark's stock figures (sources in [How the numbers were made](#how-the-numbers-were-made)). Single-core is not affected.

## Words used in this report

- **iGPU:** graphics built into the CPU, so no graphics card is needed. KF/F Intel CPUs and Ryzen 5000 have none.
- **PL1 / PL2:** the CPU's power limits, set in the BIOS. Setting both to 125–150 W keeps it cooler and safer for 24/7 use.
- **VRM:** the board's power circuit for the CPU. A small VRM gets hot at high power.
- **Microcode / ageing:** 13th/14th-gen Intel CPUs can slowly become unstable. BIOS updates with microcode 0x12F or newer prevent it, but cannot repair a damaged CPU.
- **AIO:** an all-in-one water cooler (pump + radiator).
- **CMR:** the normal hard-drive recording type, which works well with ZFS.
- **ZFS mirror:** two drives holding the same data, so one can fail without data loss.
- **ECC UDIMM, 2Rx8:** server memory sticks. On these Intel B760 boards they work as normal memory, with no error correction.
- **GOP / CSM:** a graphics card needs UEFI support (GOP) to show a picture on modern boards with legacy mode (CSM) switched off.
- **Tier 2:** a private seller in Germany, Slovenia or Hungary who would have to agree to post to Austria. Their delivery cost is a carrier-tariff **estimate**.
- **Basis of a resale value:**
  - *sold*: 3 or more recent sales of that item.
  - *thin*: 1–2 sales.
  - *ask-only*: no sales, so 85% of current asking prices.
  - *comparable*: sales of similar items.
  - *nominal*: a judgement value.
- **VB:** *Verhandlungsbasis*, meaning the price is negotiable.

## Why these choices

- **Platform: Intel LGA1700 on a DDR4 board.**
  - **Why:** it gives the fastest single-core per euro (Raptor Lake P-cores, which CST uses) and the lowest idle power (20–35 W with the iGPU), and it takes cheap DDR4.
  - **Not AM5:** Ryzen 7000/9000 has AVX-512 and twice the memory bandwidth, but every AM5 build that needs 64 GB of DDR5 bought separately came out at €1,097 or more, because 64 GB of DDR5 costs €565–680 in this market. The only cheaper AM5 route is the Villach homelab (€305–455 net if every part sells), which needs €3,000 cash and 13–15 items to sell.
  - **AM4 (Ryzen 9 5900X):** cheap and runs at full power, but 20% slower single-core and about 15–20 W more at idle (about €55–100 over 4 years).
  - **Workstations (Xeon W, Threadripper, EPYC):** more memory channels, but every one found was over €860 or below 3,000 single-core.
- **CPU: i7-13700K.** 8 P-cores for CST and 8 E-cores for VMs and background jobs. Capped at 125–150 W for a warm room, it sustains about 40–42k multi-core with 4,325 single-core. It has an iGPU, so no display card is needed.
- **RAM: 64 GB DDR4 in 4 slots.** 64 GB is your main goal, and DDR4 is the only affordable way to get it.
  - The 4×16 GB ECC kit at €105.70 is the cheapest 64 GB found this round.
  - The board takes up to 128 GB later.
  - The cost is bandwidth: 38–43 GB/s against about 90 GB/s for DDR5. Memory-bound FDTD jobs will feel it.
- **Board: Gigabyte B760M DS3H DDR4, new (€95.90).**
  - It has Q-Flash Plus, 4 DIMM slots, 2× M.2, 4× SATA and 2.5 GbE.
  - It replaces the Klagenfurt PC's DDR5-only MSI board. Keeping that board would need 64 GB of DDR5 (€565–680). The swap costs €131.60 net: €95.90 for the DS3H + €105.70 for 64 GB DDR4 − €70 for the old board.
- **Storage: NVMe boot + a ZFS mirror of two 2 TB CMR HDDs.** The PC's 500 GB NVMe holds Proxmox and the VMs; ask for its make and health first, and if it's a worn no-name drive add the €57.99 PM9A1. The near-new IronWolf Pro pair (50 h and 2 h) is NAS-rated CMR, which suits 24/7 ZFS better than the €50 cheaper WD pair (one drive at 42,180 h).
- **Cooling and PSU:**
  - An air cooler (Freezer 36) instead of the donor's water cooler, because a pump is one more thing to fail in a remote box.
  - A known, nearly new Seasonic 650 W (Bronze) instead of the donor's unbranded 750 W.
  - Your own ATX case.
- **Buying style:** the Klagenfurt PC is a prebuilt that you strip. In your preferred style (buy a prebuilt and upgrade it), B4 (the Pfunds PC with a CPU swap) is €16.45 cheaper and only 3 parcels. But it has 32 GB, a power-limited board and no mirror; B0 gets all three for that difference.

## Setup checklist for the Intel builds

1. **BIOS first.** Flash the newest BIOS before the 13th/14th-gen CPU runs any load.
   - The Gigabyte B760M DS3H DDR4 can be flashed **without a CPU** with its Q-Flash Plus button.
   - On the Pfunds MSI PRO B760M-P DDR4, flash **7E02v1J** (Intel microcode 0x137) while the i5-12400 is still fitted, then swap the CPU. 7E02v1E was the first version with 0x12F, Intel's May 2025 fix aimed at systems that run for days at low load, which describes a home server exactly.
2. **Power caps for 24/7 in Morocco.** In the BIOS, set PL1 = PL2:
   - **DS3H with a Freezer 36:** start at 125 W, and go to 150 W only if HWiNFO shows the VRM staying below about 90 °C during a long CST run. The board has 6 Vcore phases with a heatsink on only one bank; no VRM temperature test was found.
   - **Pfunds B760M-P:** about 100 W, or 125 W with a fan blowing on the VRM. It has 3 real phases, doubled, and no VRM heatsink.
   - **What the cap costs:** ComputerBase measured the i7-13700K at 88% of stock multi-core at 125 W and 92% at 142 W, and the i9-13900K at 83% and 86%. Single-core is unaffected.
3. **No Intel warranty.** Intel's warranty guide excludes used and second-hand CPUs, including the 13th/14th-gen extension.
   - Within the first days, run a stress test and an idle soak: OCCT or y-cruncher, plus a night of idling.
   - A willhaben PayLivery purchase gives you the buyer-protection window for this. A pickup does not, so test at the seller's place where you can.
4. **Display output.** The i7-13700K, i9-13900K and i9-12900K have an iGPU.
   - F/KF CPUs and Ryzen 5000 need a card with UEFI support (GOP): the €40.70 GT 1030 in the builds, or the Pfunds GTX 1650.
   - An older €10 Radeon HD 7750 was dropped. Cards from 2012 often lack GOP, so they may show no picture once CSM is off.
5. **RAM.**
   - **Kingston kit (DS3H builds):** 4×16 GB Kingston KTD-PE424E (DDR4-2400 ECC UDIMM, 2Rx8), €100 + €5.70, posted today.
     - Gigabyte's spec for the B760M DS3H DDR4 lists ECC UDIMM 1Rx8/2Rx8 as supported. It runs as normal memory, with no error correction (real ECC would need a W680 board). Bandwidth is 38.4 GB/s.
   - **If it sells, two fallbacks:**
     - a second ECC kit, 4×16 GB Samsung DDR4-2133, [willhaben 1153528073](https://www.willhaben.at/iad/object?adId=1153528073), Allerheiligen, €165.70 delivered (+€63.00 including willhaben's fee). It gives 34.1 GB/s, and the seller account is new (30 Sep 2026).
     - the Arlsdorf non-ECC kit, €192.99 (+€91.79 including willhaben's fee), 42.7 GB/s.
   - **Other boards:** the ECC kits aren't confirmed on the MSI boards (Pfunds, Dörfla) or on AM4, so those builds use the Arlsdorf kit.
   - **Bandwidth:** all of these are 17–33% below DDR4-3200/3600, which matters for FDTD.
6. **Drives.** The HDD mirror listing is one ad with several 2 TB drives, so ask the seller to put two in one PayLivery parcel (willhaben's *Preisvorschlag*).
   - WD Red Plus (42,180 h) + WD Black (16,752 h): €50 each.
   - Two near-new Seagate IronWolf Pro (50 h and 2 h): €75 each. These are better for 24/7.
7. **Running it from Singapore and flying it to Morocco.**
   - **Remote access:** reach Proxmox only through Tailscale or WireGuard.
   - **Power cuts:** in the BIOS, set *Restore on AC Power Loss* to *Power On* so it restarts after one.
   - **No remote BIOS console:** these boards have none (no IPMI/vPro). A KVM-over-IP device or a smart plug for a hard power-cycle is a sensible extra; at €800 there is money left for one.
   - **Flying:** take the Freezer 36 off the board before the flight. Carry the drives in the cabin in anti-static bags, and keep the receipts for customs.

## How the numbers were made

- **Prices and delivery:**
  - Every listing in the build cards and near-miss tables was opened and re-opened on 10 Oct 2026.
  - Delivery costs are the ad's own willhaben PayLivery option or the shop's tariff.
  - **willhaben's buyer-protection fee is included** as its own line in every build: willhaben's help page (updated 8 Oct 2026) says the buyer pays *"mindestens 0,99 € bzw. ab 4,5% vom Artikelpreis (bis max. 49,99€)"*, and its example uses 5%, so 5% (minimum €0.99) is counted on each PayLivery item. The exact fee is shown at checkout. Pickups, shops and foreign sellers have no such fee.
  - Near-miss tables do **not** yet include this fee on the add-on parts (about €10–25 per build).
  - Where an ad shows **no** delivery cost, the report says *not shown*.
  - For tier-2 sellers the carrier tariff is marked **estimate**: DHL DE→AT online is €45.49 up to 31.5 kg (€22.49 up to 10 kg), plus about €14 insurance above €500.
- **Resale:** `resale.py` combines Tradera **sold** items from the last 45 days, willhaben *reserviert* ads, and the AT/DE asking-price median as a cap.
  - The value is the sold median, capped at the ask median and rounded down to €5. Each value carries its basis label (see the word list).
  - Two or three independent checks re-derived every resold part from hand-inspected rows. Values were lowered where the rows were polluted by laptops, complete PCs or new sealed items.
- **Performance:** PassMark CPU Mark, matched by exact CPU id on cpubenchmark.net. Sustained multi-core under the cap:
  - i7-13700K: 88% of stock at 125 W, 92% at 142 W (ComputerBase).
  - i9-13900K: 83% at 125 W, 86% at 142 W (ComputerBase). The i9-14900K(F) is assumed to scale the same. pokde.net and boringtextreviews.com measured 77–82% at 125 W.
  - Ryzen 9 5900X runs at stock.
- **Idle power and electricity:**
  - Intel B760/Z690 with iGPU: 20–35 W.
  - GTX 1650: about 8 W.
  - 2-HDD mirror: +8–10 W.
  - AM4 Ryzen 9 with a small display card: about 40–55 W on X570, less on B550. This is estimated from ComputerBase's 55 W (5900X, X570) and 42 W (5950X, B550), measured with an RTX 3080 that itself idles at about 15 W.
  - At Moroccan tariffs (1.17–1.60 MAD/kWh at 11.12 MAD/EUR), each watt running 24/7 costs €0.92–1.26 a year.
- **Rules kept:**
  - every listing reported was opened;
  - no price was estimated (unknown delivery says *not shown*, and tier-2 tariffs say *estimate*);
  - nothing posted before 10 Sep 2026, sold or reserved is included;
  - no pickup-only ads outside Klagenfurt/Villach;
  - no seller was contacted.

## What changed in this round

- **Harvesting is allowed.** You can buy a complete PC just to strip it and resell what you don't need. Every budget is now **net**: purchase price + delivery − realistic resale of the parts you don't keep. The cash you need first is shown separately.
- **Resale values come from sold listings.** eBay blocks every request from this environment, so sold prices come from **Tradera**, a Swedish eBay-style auction site that shows real sale prices and dates. Sales that ended between 26 Aug and 10 Oct 2026 are converted at the ECB rate of 11.1675 SEK/EUR (9 Oct 2026). They are cross-checked with willhaben ads marked *reserviert* and capped at the current Austrian/German asking-price median, because you would be competing with those asks. Three independent checks re-derived every value. Selling through willhaben PayLivery costs a private seller nothing; the buyer pays the fee and postage.
- **Pickup in Klagenfurt and Villach now counts**, as you asked; those listings are marked *pickup by you*. Everything else must ship to Villach, and ads that are pickup-only elsewhere are still excluded.
- **Prebuilt-plus-upgrade routes are marked** because you prefer them, but every route is ranked by net cost.
- **Performance is shown as sustained.** CPUs are power-capped for 24/7 use in a warm room, so multi-core scores are derated from PassMark's stock figures (sources in [How the numbers were made](#how-the-numbers-were-made)).

## Why these choices

- **Platform: Intel LGA1700 on a DDR4 board.**
  - **Why:** it gives the fastest single-core per euro (Raptor Lake P-cores, which CST uses) and the lowest idle power (20–35 W with integrated graphics), and it takes cheap DDR4.
  - **Not AM5:** Ryzen 7000/9000 has AVX-512 and twice the memory bandwidth, but 64 GB of DDR5 costs €565–680 in this market, so every AM5 build came out at €1,097 or more.
  - **AM4 (Ryzen 9 5900X):** cheap and runs at full power, but 20% slower single-core and 20–25 W more at idle (about €75–125 over 4 years).
  - **Workstations (Xeon W, Threadripper, EPYC):** more memory channels, but every one found was either over €860 or below 3,000 single-core.
- **CPU: i7-13700K, or i9-13900K at €800.** 8 P-cores for CST and 8 E-cores for VMs and background jobs.
  - Even capped at 125–150 W for a warm room it sustains about 40–42k multi-core, with 4,325 single.
  - The i9-13900K adds about 15–20% multi-core for about €21 net. It also carries more ageing exposure.
  - The i7-13700K in the Klagenfurt PC comes with integrated graphics, so no display card is needed.
- **RAM: 64 GB DDR4 in 4 slots.** 64 GB is your main goal and DDR4 is the only affordable way to get it.
  - The 4×16 GB ECC kit at €105.70 is the cheapest 64 GB found anywhere in this round (the B760M DS3H runs it in non-ECC mode).
  - The board takes up to 128 GB later (4×32 GB).
  - The cost is bandwidth: DDR4-2400/2666 is 38–43 GB/s, against about 90 GB/s for DDR5. FDTD jobs that are memory-bound will feel it.
- **Board: Gigabyte B760M DS3H DDR4, new (€95.90).**
  - Q-Flash Plus lets you flash the newest microcode without a CPU.
  - It has 4 DIMM slots, 2× M.2, 4× SATA and 2.5 GbE.
  - Its 6-phase VRM is fine at a 125–150 W cap.
  - It replaces DDR5 boards from donor PCs because their DDR5 RAM is worth more sold (about €400 for 32 GB) than DDR4 costs.
- **Storage: NVMe boot + a ZFS mirror of two 2 TB CMR HDDs.**
  - The donor PC's 500 GB NVMe holds Proxmox and the VMs.
  - The near-new IronWolf Pro pair (50 h and 2 h) is CMR and NAS-rated, which suits 24/7 ZFS better than the 42,000-hour WD Red Plus pair that is €50 cheaper.
- **Cooling and PSU:**
  - An air cooler (Freezer 36) instead of the donor's AIO, because a pump is one more thing to fail in a remote box.
  - A known, nearly new Seasonic 650 W (Bronze) instead of the donor's unbranded 750 W.
  - Your own ATX case.
- **Buying style:** a prebuilt-plus-upgrade route (the Pfunds PC) is in every report, as you prefer, but the Klagenfurt PC wins by enough to lead. It needs one trip to Klagenfurt and a few Austrian parcels.

## Setup checklist for the Intel builds

1. **BIOS first.** Flash the newest BIOS before the 13th/14th-gen CPU runs any load.
   - The Gigabyte B760M DS3H DDR4 can be flashed **without a CPU** using its Q-Flash Plus button.
   - On the Pfunds MSI PRO B760M-P DDR4, flash **7E02v1J** (Intel microcode 0x137) while the i5-12400 is still fitted, then swap the CPU. 7E02v1E was the first version with 0x12F, Intel's May 2025 fix for the Raptor Lake ageing problem. That fix targets systems that run for days at low load, which is exactly a home server.
2. **Power caps for 24/7 in Morocco.** In the BIOS, set PL1 = PL2:
   - DS3H with a Freezer 36: 125–150 W. The board has 6 real Vcore phases and a heatsink on only one VRM bank.
   - Pfunds B760M-P: about 100 W, or 125 W with a fan blowing on the VRM. It has 3 real phases, doubled, and no VRM heatsink.
   - What it costs: ComputerBase measured the i7-13700K at 92% of stock multi-core at 142 W and 88% at 125 W. Single-core is unaffected.
3. **No Intel warranty.** Intel's warranty guide excludes used and second-hand CPUs, including the 13th/14th-gen extension. Within the first days, run a stress test and an idle soak (OCCT or y-cruncher, plus a night idling). A willhaben PayLivery purchase gives you the buyer-protection window for this; a pickup does not, so test at the seller's place where you can.
4. **Display output.** The i7-13700K, i9-13900K and i9-12900K have graphics built in. **F/KF CPUs and Ryzen 5000 need a display card that supports UEFI (GOP).**
   - The old €10 HD 7750 found earlier has no UEFI GOP, so it shows no picture on these boards once legacy boot (CSM) is off. It has been removed.
   - Use the GT 1030 listed in the builds, or keep the Pfunds GTX 1650.
5. **RAM.** There are two 64 GB kits, and the builds say which one they use.
   - **The cheaper kit (DS3H builds):** 4×16 GB Kingston KTD-PE424E (DDR4-2400 ECC unbuffered, 2Rx8), €100 + €5.70, posted today. Gigabyte's spec for the B760M DS3H DDR4 lists ECC unbuffered DIMMs 1Rx8/2Rx8 as supported, running in non-ECC mode. It gives 38.4 GB/s.
     - Only one such kit exists. If it sells, use the Arlsdorf kit (+€87.29), or a 2×16 kit with 2 slots free.
     - On the MSI boards (Pfunds, Dörfla) and on AM4 it isn't confirmed, so those builds use the Arlsdorf kit.
   - **The Arlsdorf kit:** 4×16 GB SK hynix HMA82GU6CJR8N-VK (non-ECC, DDR4-2666), €192.99. It runs at 2666 with no XMP: 42.7 GB/s, about 10% more than the ECC kit.
   - **Bandwidth:** both are 17–33% below a DDR4-3200/3600 kit, which matters for FDTD. Manual tuning above the rated speed may work but isn't guaranteed with four dual-rank sticks.
6. **Drives.** The HDD mirror listing is one ad with several 2 TB drives, so ask the seller to combine two drives into one PayLivery parcel (willhaben's *Preisvorschlag*).
   - WD Red Plus (42,180 h) + WD Black (16,752 h): €50 each.
   - Two near-new Seagate IronWolf Pro (50 h and 2 h): €75 each. Better for 24/7.

## How the numbers were made

- **Prices and delivery:** every listing was opened and re-opened on 10 Oct 2026. Delivery costs are the ad's own willhaben PayLivery option or the shop's tariff.
  - Where an ad shows **no** shipping cost, the report says *not shown*.
  - For German/Slovenian private sellers (tier 2), the DHL/Pošta tariff is marked **estimate**: DHL DE→AT online is €45.49 up to 30 kg, plus about €14 insurance above €500.
- **Resale (sold-data method):** `resale.py` collects:
  1. Tradera **sold** items from the last 45 days;
  2. willhaben *reserviert* ads;
  3. the AT/DE asking-price median, used as a cap.

  The value is the sold median, capped at the ask median and rounded down to €5. Where fewer than 3 sales exist, the basis column says *thin* or *ask-only* (0.85 × median ask), and those amounts are totalled in each build's table. Every resold part was re-derived by two to three independent checks with hand-inspected rows; values were lowered where the rows were polluted by laptops, complete PCs or new sealed items.
- **Performance:** PassMark CPU Mark, matched by exact CPU id on cpubenchmark.net. Sustained multi-core under a power cap:
  - i7-13700K: ComputerBase measured 88% of stock at 125 W and 92% at 142 W.
  - i9-13900K/14900K: about 83–86% at 125–150 W (ComputerBase; pokde.net and boringtextreviews.com measured 77–82% at 125 W).
  - Ryzen 9 5900X: runs at stock.
- **Idle power and electricity:** idle ranges follow published measurements:
  - Intel B760/Z690 with integrated graphics: 20–35 W;
  - GTX 1650: about 8 W;
  - a 2-HDD mirror: +8–10 W;
  - AM4 Ryzen 9 with a display card: 45–65 W.

  At Moroccan tariffs (1.17–1.60 MAD/kWh at 11.12 MAD/EUR), each watt running 24/7 costs €0.92–1.26 a year.
- **Rules kept:**
  - every listing reported was opened;
  - no price was estimated (unknowns say *not shown*);
  - nothing posted before 10 Sep 2026, sold or reserved is included;
  - no pickup-only ads outside Klagenfurt/Villach;
  - no seller was contacted.
